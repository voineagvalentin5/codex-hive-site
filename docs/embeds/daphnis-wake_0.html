Reading the picture

Why the flow runs both ways

Orbital speed falls with distance from Saturn. In the moon's frame, particles outside its orbit drift backward and particles inside drift forward, at 1.5 Ω|x₀|. Daphnis is the fixed point in the middle.

Where the wavelength comes from

A passing particle is pulled once, then oscillates radially at the epicyclic frequency, which equals Ω for a Kepler orbit. In one oscillation it drifts 2π/Ω × 1.5Ω|x₀| = 3π|x₀| past the moon. Farther streamlines drift faster, so their ripples are longer.

Size of the wiggle

For weak encounters the amplitude is about 2.25 μ/x₀², where μ = G·M/Ω². It grows with the moon's mass and falls as 1/x₀². The readouts compare the simulation with this linear estimate, which loses accuracy for close, heavy encounters.

What this model leaves out

Collisions between particles, which damp the ripples downstream, ring self-gravity, and Daphnis's small orbital tilt, which adds the vertical ripples Cassini saw near equinox.

Daphnis mass 6.8×10¹³ kg and orbital radius 136,505 km (Wikipedia, citing Weiss et al. 2009) give a Hill radius near 4.7 km. Integration is 4th-order Runge-Kutta with a lightly softened moon potential.
